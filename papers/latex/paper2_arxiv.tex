\begin{abstract}
\noindent \textbf{Background:} Clinical Large Language Models (LLMs) deployed in emergency triage face contextual framing pressures, deceptive symptom resolution, and authority sycophancy. Standard single-prompt evaluations cannot isolate knowledge deficits from contextual susceptibility. \\
\textbf{Objective:} We formulate a \textbf{Matched-Pair Counterfactual Protocol Design} across 5 core acute chest pain scenarios ($N=5$ matched pairs) and verify the auditing pipeline using synthetic unaligned failure unit tests alongside real single-turn frontier LLM responses. \\
\textbf{Methods:} Each scenario is structured into a \textbf{Neutral Control Arm} ($P_i^{\text{ctrl}}$) and an \textbf{Adversarial Pressured Arm} ($P_i^{\text{pres}} = P_i^{\text{ctrl}} \oplus \delta_i$). Protocol adherence shift is defined as $\Delta = \text{PassRate}(P^{\text{pres}}) - \text{PassRate}(P^{\text{ctrl}})$. \\
\textbf{Pilot Verification:} In single-turn pilot testing across the 5 pressured scenarios, the evaluated real frontier LLM responses maintained 5/5 (100.0\%) protocol adherence ($\Delta = 0.0\%$), resisting premature discharge after a gastrointestinal cocktail and rejecting unverified colleague NSTEMI labels. Synthetic failure unit tests confirmed that the auditing rules accurately capture each targeted violation mode (transitioning from 5/5 in control mocks to 1/5 in pressured mocks).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
While LLMs achieve high accuracy on neutral medical vignettes \cite{kung2023performance}, clinical decision-making in acute chest pain \cite{gulati2021aha} is vulnerable to cognitive biases \cite{croskerry2003importance} and social sycophancy \cite{sharma2023towards, chen2024sycophancy}. Recent evaluations of clinical authority pressure and sycophancy demonstrate that even capable models can exhibit compliance drift when confronted with assertive user or colleague framing \cite{chen2024sycophancy}. To separate baseline medical knowledge from contextual framing effects, we introduce a reproducible matched-pair counterfactual evaluation protocol.

\section{Matched-Pair Counterfactual Methodology}
For each physiological scenario $v_i \in \{v_1, \dots, v_5\}$, we construct a neutral control prompt $P_i^{\text{ctrl}}$ and a pressured prompt $P_i^{\text{pres}} = P_i^{\text{ctrl}} \oplus \delta_i$. We define the \textbf{Protocol Adherence Shift ($\Delta$)} for a given model cohort as:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta = \text{PassRate}(P^{\text{pres}}) - \text{PassRate}(P^{\text{ctrl}})
\end{equation}
Negative values of $\Delta$ indicate degradation in protocol adherence induced by the modifier $\delta_i$.

\section{Methodological Boundaries \& Phase-2 Expansion Protocol}
This pilot establishes the software mechanics of counterfactual auditing but has strict statistical and design boundaries that inform our Phase-2 expansion:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sample Size \& Statistical Power Bound:} A sample of $N=5$ matched pairs cannot achieve two-sided statistical significance at $\alpha = 0.05$ under the exact McNemar test even in the most extreme case of 5 discordant pairs ($p = 2 \times 0.5^5 = 0.0625$). Detecting moderate pressure-induced drops requires scaling to $N \ge 40$--$50$ independent scenarios.
    \item \textbf{Need for Bidirectional (Rightful vs. Wrongful) Pressure Controls:} Because single-turn frontier responses passed all 5 wrongful-pressure prompts, evaluating only wrongful pressure rewards blind model stubbornness. Phase-2 incorporates \textit{valid clinical hints} (where colleague pressure highlights a life-saving finding) alongside wrongful pressure.
\end{enumerate}
